# Editorial policy

HISTORYVERSE must be trustworthy. These rules apply to people, to automation and to AI equally.

## Accuracy and sourcing

1. **Never fabricate** facts, quotations, events, dates or references.
2. Every published claim must be traceable to at least one cited source. Contested claims cite each position.
3. Wikidata and Wikipedia are **research inputs, not final authorities**. Prefer independent scholarly, archival and primary sources where available, and record provenance for every imported fact.
4. **Preserve uncertainty.** Approximate dates are marked "c.", doubtful ones "(uncertain)", and disputed relationships or borders are labelled as disputed.
5. **Distinguish fact from interpretation.** Interpretive passages are written as such ("Historians such as … argue that …").
6. Represent disputed history and competing national, ethnic or religious narratives **fairly**, with sources.
7. Do not claim global completeness. Coverage grows from sourced material.

## AI-generated material

- AI drafts must pass **Research Review → Editorial Approval** before publication.
- AI reconstructions always carry the label **"AI-Generated Historical Reconstruction — Not an Authentic Photograph"**, which uses the reserved `reconstruction` colour, and are never presented as archival evidence.
- Visual details unsupported by evidence are treated as interpretation in prompts and captions.
- YouTube uploads use the platform's altered or synthetic content disclosure whenever realistic synthetic media is included.
- No cloning of real voices without documented authorization.

## Copyright and licensing

- Online images are **not** assumed to be free to reuse. Each asset records its title, date, creator, source URL, licence, required attribution and the subjects it relates to.
- Assets with unknown or restrictive rights are not published.
- Copyrighted text is not copied. Summarise and cite instead.

## Workflow

`Draft → Research Review → Editorial Approval → Published → Updated`

Every state change and edit is recorded in revision history and the audit log. Public publishing (site or YouTube) **always** requires explicit approval from an authorized editor. Readers can submit correction requests, which enter the review queue.
